=== FINANCING OPTIONS REPORT ===
Assumptions: $65,000 drive-away (VIC representative); PAYG senior IT (high marginal rate ~32.5% + Medicare 2%); 5-year view; EV eligible for FBT exemption.

1. Standard secured car loan
- How: Bank/lender secures loan against car; fixed or variable rate.
- Terms: 1–7 yr; rates ~6.5–8.5% p.a. (compare on RateCity / Canstar, 2026); $65k at 7.5% over 5 yr = ~$1,302/mo; total interest ~$13,120. No tax benefit for PAYG (interest not deductible).
- Pros: Simple; predictable; car ownership; no job-dependency.
- Cons: Higher after-tax cost; no FBT benefit; depreciation risk.
- Best when: You want full ownership; employer won’t support novated lease; rate < home loan rate.
- Caveats: Early payout fees (some lenders); secured asset risk.

2. Home loan top-up
- How: Add $65k to owner-occupied mortgage; extra repayments to clear in ~5 yr.
- Terms: Mortgage rate ~5.5–6.5% (variable, 2026); $65k extra at 6.0% with extra payments = ~$1,260/mo to clear in 5 yr; interest ~$10,300.
- Tax: No deduction for private vehicle (not investment property use). If property is investment, apportion — complex; seek accountant.
- Pros: Lowest rate; flexible repayments; no separate loan fee.
- Cons: Extends mortgage; risk of not making extra payments; not tax-efficient.
- Best when: You have equity; will stick to extra payments; rate significantly below car loan.
- Caveats: LMI if LVR >80%; if you skip extra payments, cost rises sharply.

3. Novated lease (EV, FBT-exempt eligible)
- How: Employer leases car; payments pre-tax; FBT exemption applies (ATO). Bundled running costs (fuel/electricity, rego, insurance, servicing) can be included.
- Terms: 3–5 yr; residual 30–45%; for $65k EV, pre-tax payment ~$900–$1,050/mo (est.); after-tax cashflow often lower than car loan due to tax saving. FBT exemption means $0 FBT (for eligible EV under LCT threshold).
- Tax: PAYG saves marginal rate (32.5% + 2% Medicare = 34.5%) on pre-tax portion. Example: $1,000/mo pre-tax ≈ $655/mo after-tax for employee in 34.5% bracket.
- Pros: Tax-efficient; bundled costs; FBT exemption for eligible EV; no upfront GST cost (leased); simple salary deduction.
- Cons: Not your asset (residual applies); job-change risk (must settle or transfer); residual risk if car value < residual; must check LCT threshold eligibility; admin fee (~$10–$20/mo).
- Best when: Employer supports; EV under LCT threshold; you want tax optimisation and bundled running costs.
- Caveats: Confirm employer’s novated provider (e.g., SG Fleet, Maxxia); verify residual percentage; check FBT exemption applies to exact model and price; job change requires plan.

4. Green car loan / dealer finance / balloon / unsecured
- Green loan: Similar to secured car loan; some banks (e.g., CommBank, Bank Australia) offer lower rate for EV (~6.0–7.5%); check terms.
- Dealer/manufacturer finance: Tesla/BYD/Kia may offer 0% or low APR promotions; compare total cost (often shorter terms, balloon, or higher price offset). Always compare drive-away total, not just rate.
- Balloon (residual) loan: Lower monthly payments; large final payment; risk if car value < balloon; common in dealer finance.
- Unsecured personal loan: Higher rate (~8–14%); no security; quick approval; generally not best for $65k car.
